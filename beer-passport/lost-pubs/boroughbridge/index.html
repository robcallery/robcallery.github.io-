The Lost Pubs of North Yorkshire / Boroughbridge

The Lost Pubs of Boroughbridge

Every pub has a story. Some have simply stopped serving.

A town built around the road, the river and the inn

For centuries, Boroughbridge was much more than a Yorkshire market town. Its position on the Great North Road made it an important stopping point for coaches, travellers and horses moving between northern and southern England.

The River Ure brought additional trade, while cattle drovers, horse dealers and visitors to local fairs all contributed to the town’s busy streets.

At its peak, Boroughbridge supported 22 inns. The Crown alone could stable around 100 horses, illustrating the scale of the coaching trade.

During the busy droving season, as many as 2,000 cattle a day crossed the bridge on their way from Scotland to southern markets.

Then the railways changed the town. By 1853, stagecoach transport had disappeared and seven of the original 22 inns had already been lost.

Some former inns became shops or homes. Others disappeared altogether. Several survived and continued to welcome customers.

Today, traces of this history remain hidden in old buildings, street names and the stories of local families.

This archive aims to recover those stories, one pub at a time.

Source: The History of Boroughbridge

1700 to today

The road, the river and the railway, and how the inns fared. Only documented dates are shown; general events are never used to guess when a particular pub opened or closed.

  1. 1776Parliament authorises six locks to make the River Ure navigable as far as Ripon.

    Source: Langthorpe Loop heritage walking guide

  2. 1823Baines's directory lists the Blue Bell and White Horse at Kirby Hill, and the Fox & Hounds and Red Lion at Langthorpe.

    Source: Baines's Directory, 1823: Kirby Hill parish trades and professions

  3. 1847The River Ure Navigation, insolvent, is sold to the Leeds and Thirsk Railway Company.

    Source: The History of Boroughbridge

  4. 1848The railway arrives, with a station on the Milby road. (Wikipedia gives 17 June 1847 for the first station.)

    Sources: The History of Boroughbridge; Langthorpe Loop heritage walking guide; Boroughbridge railway station

  5. 1853Stagecoach transport has disappeared, and seven of the town's 22 inns have been lost.

    Source: The History of Boroughbridge

  6. 1875A second station opens on 1 April.

    Source: Boroughbridge railway station

  7. 1890Bulmer's directory lists the Anchor, Fox and Hounds, Old Red Lion, Grantham Arms, Blue Bell, and the Ship Inn and Railway Tavern beerhouses.

    Source: Bulmer's Directory, 1890: Kirby Hill parish trades and professions

  8. c.1930Around this year the Three Horse Shoes is rebuilt as a roadhouse for the Great North Road.

    Source: The Three Horse Shoes Public House, list entry 1245941

  9. 1945The A1 bridge over the Ure collapses; the army puts up a Bailey bridge.

    Source: Boroughbridge

  10. 1950Passenger trains stop calling at Boroughbridge on 25 September.

    Source: Boroughbridge railway station

  11. 1962The Great North Road is moved west to become the A1. (The town council's history gives 1963 for the bypass.)

    Sources: Langthorpe Loop heritage walking guide; The History of Boroughbridge

  12. 1964The station closes completely.

    Source: Boroughbridge railway station

  13. 2005Eight pubs or hotels are still trading in the town.

    Source: The History of Boroughbridge

  14. 2010Bernard Porter compiles Hostelries in Boroughbridge, recording 36 sites and what their buildings were then used for.

    Source: Bernard Porter, Hostelries in Boroughbridge (February 2010)

  15. 2011The Malt Shovel closes permanently on 1 January, according to CAMRA.

    Source: Pubs in Boroughbridge

  16. 2026The Wild Swan at Minskip closes suddenly in January. In October the Tap on the Tutt closes for refurbishment.

    Sources: Wild Swan at Minskip closes (8 January 2026); Tap on the Tutt, Boroughbridge

Walk the history

Five short walks linking sites whose locations are documented, along public streets. The dotted line joins the stops in order and isn't an exact walking route.

Bridge Street and the River Ure

  1. Three Horse Shoes
  2. Blinking Owl
  3. Crown Hotel

Start by the Ure at the Borough Bridge. The Three Horse Shoes and Riverside Court (once the Blinking Owl) face each other on Bridge Street. Walk south up Bridge Street to the Crown at the junction with Horsefair and Fishergate.

Horsefair and St James Square

  1. Crown Hotel
  2. Three Greyhounds
  3. Black Bull Inn

From the Crown, the old post office building next door on Horsefair is the listed former annexe of the Three Greyhounds Hotel. Return to the Crown and follow Fishergate and the High Street south to St James Square for the Black Bull.

High Street and the coaching inns

  1. Crown Hotel
  2. Black Bull Inn

The Crown and the Black Bull bookend the High Street. The Black Horse, Black Swan, Saddle and White Hart all stood on this street, but their exact buildings are not yet confirmed, so they are not marked. Look up as you walk.

Langthorpe and Milby

  1. Grantham Arms
  2. Anchor Inn
  3. Fox and Hounds

Cross the Borough Bridge north. The Grantham Arms is on the Milby side; the Anchor stands on Leeming Lane; and the Fox and Hounds is west along Skelton Road. The Langthorpe Loop guide from the town council covers the brewery buildings in more detail.

Kirby Hill and the old Great North Road

  1. Blue Bell Inn
  2. Great North Road milestone

The Blue Bell stands on Leeming Lane, the old Great North Road, north of Langthorpe. The listed milestone is about 10 metres south of the pub.

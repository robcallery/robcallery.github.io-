Safe Standing in the South Stand – Has Anyone Actually Sat Down in There?

By Rob Callery · 28 October 2026 · Leeds United

Leeds United have announced that safe standing is coming to the Norman Hunter South Stand at Elland Road.

Which raises one very important question…

When was the last time anybody actually sat down in the South Stand?

Let’s face it, the seats have spent most of their existence folded up, gathering dust and wondering what their purpose in life actually is.

The club is essentially spending money making something official that South Stand regulars have been doing for years.

Joking aside, it’s a welcome safety improvement, and one that’s probably long overdue.

Standing room only… well, almost

Anyone who’s been to Elland Road knows that sitting down isn’t exactly a priority when Leeds are playing.

Particularly in the South Stand.

Whether it’s celebrating a last-minute winner, shouting at the referee for a decision that was clearly wrong (even if the replay suggests otherwise), or joining in with Marching On Together, you’re probably going to spend a fair amount of the afternoon on your feet.

And that’s part of what makes Elland Road special.

It’s never been the most glamorous stadium in the country. The concourses aren’t exactly luxurious, the facilities have seen better days, and depending on where you’re sitting, getting to the toilet at half-time can feel like an expedition.

But would you swap it?

Not a chance.

So what’s actually changing?

The club has confirmed that rail seating is being installed across the Norman Hunter South Stand following advice from the Sports Grounds Safety Authority.

The installation is being completed in stages, with the first sections expected to be ready for the Tottenham Hotspur match on 7 November.

The current schedule is:

7 November – Tottenham Hotspur: The upper tier and lower-tier sections FA1 and FA6 are due to be completed.

28 November – Coventry City: The remaining lower-tier sections are scheduled to be ready.

Leeds have already introduced rail seating in other parts of Elland Road, including areas of the North Stand and away section.

Now it’s the South Stand’s turn.

One thing worth mentioning is that Elland Road remains an all-seater stadium. The new rails are primarily there to improve safety when supporters stand, rather than giving everyone an unrestricted licence to remain on their feet for 90 minutes.

Although I suspect convincing the South Stand to sit down might be a bigger challenge than getting a referee to admit he’s made a mistake.

You can’t manufacture the Elland Road atmosphere

I’ve been going to Elland Road long enough to know that there are few places quite like it when the crowd gets going.

The moment Marching On Together starts, the whole place comes alive.

And when Leeds score?

Absolute chaos.

Strangers hugging each other, people losing their voices, and somebody three rows behind you confidently explaining how they knew the goal was coming all along.

Then, five minutes later, the same bloke is telling everyone we’re going down because somebody misplaced a five-yard pass.

That’s Leeds United.

It’s emotional, irrational, occasionally exhausting and absolutely brilliant.

The South Stand has always played a huge part in that atmosphere, alongside the Kop.

And that’s why I think safe standing is a positive development.

It doesn’t create the atmosphere. The supporters do that.

But it provides a safer environment for the moments when thousands of people naturally get to their feet.

Bigger stadium, but will it still feel like Elland Road?

Of course, safe standing is only one small part of the much bigger changes happening at Elland Road.

Leeds are working towards increasing the stadium’s capacity to around 53,000, with major redevelopment plans for the West and North Stands.

And while I’m excited about the prospect of a bigger Elland Road, I do have one concern.

Please don’t turn it into another soulless modern football stadium.

You know the sort.

Beautiful concourses, enormous television screens, fancy hospitality areas and probably somebody trying to sell you a £7 artisan sausage roll.

All very impressive.

But sometimes the atmosphere is about as intimidating as a Sunday afternoon in a garden centre.

Elland Road has never needed any of that to be special.

It’s got history. It’s got character. And it’s got supporters who can make 35,000 people sound like twice that number when things are going well.

Admittedly, when things aren’t going well, those same supporters can make their feelings known just as loudly.

But that’s part of the charm.

More seats and improved facilities are absolutely welcome, especially when getting tickets has become so difficult.

I just hope the club remembers that the atmosphere is one of the biggest assets we’ve got.

Because there’s no point building a 53,000-capacity stadium if you lose the very thing that makes people want to be there.

Safe standing isn’t about going backwards

It’s important to remember why football stadiums moved towards all-seater accommodation in the first place.

There are serious historical reasons behind those changes, and supporter safety must always come first.

But rail seating isn’t about recreating the old terraces.

It’s about recognising the reality of modern football crowds and making sure supporters have appropriate safety measures around them.

Sturdy barriers help reduce the risk of supporters falling forwards when the crowd suddenly surges during celebrations.

And considering how quickly everyone jumps up when Leeds score, that’s hardly a bad idea.

Football has changed enormously over the years, but one thing hasn’t.

Supporters are still going to leap out of their seats when something exciting happens.

Even if, in the South Stand’s case, they were never sitting down to begin with.

My verdictI’m all for it.

Safe standing in the South Stand feels like a sensible improvement that acknowledges how supporters actually experience football at Elland Road.

It’s not going to magically improve performances on the pitch, stop VAR making questionable decisions or prevent somebody behind you from spending 90 minutes shouting instructions that Daniel Farke definitely can’t hear.

But it should make the stand safer, and that’s what matters.

As the redevelopment continues, I hope Leeds can strike the right balance between modernising Elland Road and protecting its character.

Give us better facilities.

Give us more seats.

Give more supporters the opportunity to watch Leeds United.

But please, whatever happens, don’t take away the atmosphere.

Because Elland Road isn’t special because of its seats, its concourses or its hospitality boxes.

It’s special because of the people who fill it every matchday.

And speaking of seats…

Maybe the club could auction off the old ones from the South Stand.

After all, most of them are practically brand new.

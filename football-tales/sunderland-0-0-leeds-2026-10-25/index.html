Sunderland 0⁠–⁠0 Leeds United: A point earned, but two points left behind?

By Rob Callery · Sunday 25 October 2026 · Stadium of Light

Leeds United made the trip to Wearside on Sunday afternoon and came away with a point following a goalless draw against Sunderland. It certainly wasn’t a classic, but in difficult weather conditions, Daniel Farke’s side showed enough defensive resilience to earn another valuable Premier League point.

The frustrating thing? This was a game Leeds could have won.

A scrappy opening 45 minutes

With Storm Boelo causing havoc across the North East, conditions at the Stadium of Light were hardly ideal for flowing Premier League football.

Sunderland started the brighter of the two sides, with Brian Brobbey proving a handful for the Leeds defence.

Fortunately, James Trafford was in excellent form. The Leeds goalkeeper produced two important first-half saves to deny Brobbey and ensure the sides went into the break level.

At the other end, Leeds struggled to create clear-cut opportunities. Dominic Calvert-Lewin worked hard up front, but Sunderland defended stubbornly.

It wasn’t pretty, and the first half certainly won’t be remembered as a Premier League classic.

Leeds grow into the game

The second half saw Leeds become more threatening, although Sunderland continued to cause problems.

Trafford was called upon again, denying Nilson Angulo twice as the hosts searched for the breakthrough.

Leeds gradually found their rhythm, with Robin Roefs having to deal with efforts from Tarik Muharemović and Ao Tanaka.

There was also a controversial moment involving Calvert-Lewin, who went down in the Sunderland penalty area following a challenge from Roefs.

Leeds appealed for a penalty, but referee Stuart Attwell waved away the claims, with VAR supporting the on-field decision.

It was one of those decisions that will certainly have divided opinion among the Leeds supporters.

Late pressure, but no winner

Farke turned to his bench as Leeds looked to find a way through.

Noah Okafor and Anton Stach entered the action midway through the second half, while Lukas Nmecha and Sean Longstaff were introduced late on.

Longstaff, a former Newcastle United player, was unsurprisingly greeted by boos from the Sunderland supporters.

He nearly had the last laugh.

The midfielder forced Roefs into an excellent late save before seeing another attempt blocked on the goal line.

It was probably the closest Leeds came to stealing all three points, but the Sunderland defence held firm.

The final whistle confirmed a 0–0 draw.

Four saves and his third Premier League clean sheet of the season.

If there was one Leeds player who could hold his head particularly high, it was James Trafford.

The goalkeeper made four saves, kept his third Premier League clean sheet of the season and ensured Sunderland couldn’t capitalise on their opportunities.

Leeds weren’t always convincing going forward, but Trafford provided the security needed at the back.

The defensive unit also deserves credit for standing firm in challenging conditions.

A good point or a missed opportunity?

That’s the big question.

An away point in the Premier League should never be dismissed, particularly on an afternoon when the weather made life difficult for both sides.

But Sunderland came into this fixture sitting bottom of the table, and Leeds will surely feel they had an opportunity to take all three points.

The positives are obvious: another clean sheet, a solid defensive performance and a point that moves Leeds into seventh place.

The negatives? Another match where the attacking quality wasn’t quite there, despite opportunities to win it late on.

Five draws from eight Premier League games tells its own story. Leeds are proving difficult to beat, but turning some of those draws into victories will be essential if they want to continue climbing the table.

Final thoughts

Sunderland 0–0 Leeds United.

It wasn’t a thriller. It wasn’t particularly entertaining. And it certainly wasn’t the sort of attacking display Leeds supporters would have been hoping for.

But sometimes football is about grinding out results.

Trafford was excellent, the defence stood firm and Leeds returned to Yorkshire with another point on the board.

Still, it’s difficult not to look at those late opportunities and wonder what might have been.

My verdict: A decent away point, but this feels like two points left behind.
